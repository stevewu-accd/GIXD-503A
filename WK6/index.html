I will select 3 interesting quotes from each article and write a little bit about it.

Programming Design Systems

"Meta-design is much more difficult than design; it is easier to draw something than to explain how to draw it."
Donald Knuth (1986), The Metafont Book

Rune included this quote in the "What is design system?" section of the book. I found the term "Meta-design" very interesting. It precisely represented the function of Design System, which is designing for designs. In this case, I believe designers that have the ability to create design systems should also be called "Meta-Designers"😎.


"Essentially, Munsell realized that his color solid had to have an irregular shape to fit his colors."
Rune Madsen, Programming Design Systems

It's very interesting to see the color theory developed throughout the history. Looking at Munsell's model, I realized the difference between human's preception and the scientific representation of colors. Which proved Rune's argument of how our color picker has flaws.


"A good analogy to consider when working with saturations of color is the way many countries have distinct ways of painting houses in their cities. The saturations of these paints can vary greatly, and although these colors do not have any inherent meaning, they do say something about the time, place, and people."
Rune Madsen, Programming Design Systems

The cultural factor of the color preception is an interesting topic. It's fascinating to dig deeper into the history of these color choices and how they affected the formation of the culture. On the other hand, how the preception of these color are changed because of the communication between cultures is also worth researching.

What do Prototypes Prototype?

"Is a brick a prototype? The answer depends on how it is used. If it is used to represent the weight and scale of some future artifact, then it certainly is: it prototypes the weight and scale of the artifact."
Stephanie Houde and Charles Hill, What do Prototypes Prototype?

The authors used the example of the brick to explain that the significance lies not in the materials or tools used to create a representation, but in how that representation is employed by a designer to explore or demonstrate some aspect of the future artifact. It expanded the definition of prototypes is fundamentally "any representation of a design idea, regardless of medium"


"No single prototype could have represented the design of the future artifact at that time. The evolving design was too fuzzy—existing mainly as a shared concept in the minds of the designers. There were also too many open and interdependent questions in every design dimension: role, look and feel, implementation."
Stephanie Houde and Charles Hill, What do Prototypes Prototype?

This quote explains why the three-dimensional model is a practical necessity rather than just a theoretical framework. It addresses how a single prototype is often impossible to represent the complexity of the system during the formative stages of a project.


"Implementation prototypes can be hard to build, and since they actually work, it is common for them to find their way directly into the final system. Two problems arise from this dynamic: firstly, programs developed mainly to demonstrate feasibility may turn out in the long term to be difficult to maintain and develop; and secondly, their temporary user interfaces may never be properly redesigned before the final system is released."
Stephanie Houde and Charles Hill, What do Prototypes Prototype?

I find it really relatable to the senarios addressed in this quote. I often find myself building a prototype just for demonstration and end up using it as the final outcome because I want to keep the fesability of the prototype. Which completely altered the purpose of creating a prototype as a test or example.
